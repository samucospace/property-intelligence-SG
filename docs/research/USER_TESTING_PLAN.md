# Singapore Home Intel: recruitment and usability testing pack

Prepared 7 October 2026 from the current product specification and interface source. These are provisional recruitment personas, not findings from user research. Validate and revise them after interviews.

## Research objective

Can people use Singapore Home Intel to answer a real private residential property question, explain the evidence they found, and recognise what the evidence cannot establish?

Test discovery, search and filters, transaction interpretation, rental and yield interpretation, map and amenity understanding, and recovery from restrictive searches. This round covers the private, read-only analytics pilot. Newsletter, contact capture and agent referrals are outside this round.

## Recruitment personas

Recruit by recent behaviour and decision context, rather than age, job title or assumed personality. A participant may fit several personas; assign one primary group for quotas.

Optional second round: two practising agents who regularly research comparable transactions. Keep their findings separate from consumer results because their vocabulary and workflows differ. Prioritise buyers and family upgraders first if only six sessions are feasible.

Across the 12 participants, aim for six using their usual phone and six using their usual computer, at least four with limited property-data experience, and at least two who normally use enlarged text, keyboard navigation, or assistive technology. These overlap the persona quotas. Do not ask participants to pretend to have an access need. Broader accessibility coverage may require additional sessions.

Avoid recruiting only friends, property enthusiasts or product insiders. Recruit through independent community groups, relocation networks and landlord groups where recruitment is permitted. Track the recruitment source so one channel does not dominate the sample.

## Recruitment message

We're looking for people who have recently researched buying, renting or letting a private residential property in Singapore to try a property research website. The session takes about 45 minutes and can be done remotely on your usual phone or computer. You don't need property expertise. We'll ask you to work through a few realistic research tasks and tell us what makes sense or feels confusing. We're testing the website, not you. This is research, not a property sales call. Reply with whether you're buying, renting, investing or letting a property, and the device you normally use for research.

Add the actual incentive, booking details and research contact before posting. Do not promise an incentive that has not been arranged.

## Screener

1. Which best describes your current situation: first private-home purchase, moving/upgrading your home, investment research, letting a property, renting/relocating, or none of these?
2. When did you last research a Singapore private residential property, and what decision were you trying to make? Ask for one concrete example, without requesting an exact home address.
3. When do you expect to make that decision: within three months, three to six months, six to 12 months, later, or no planned decision?
4. Which resources did you use, and what did you actually do with them? Look for evidence of behaviour rather than enthusiasm alone.
5. How comfortable are you reading transaction charts and price-per-square-foot figures: unfamiliar, somewhat comfortable, or very comfortable?
6. What device do you normally use? Do you use enlarged text, keyboard navigation or other access settings we should accommodate?
7. Do you work in property, valuation, market research or property software? Have you helped build or already extensively used this app?
8. Are you available for a 45-minute session? Are you comfortable sharing your screen? Ask separately about recording; allow an unrecorded session if feasible.

Select participants with a concrete recent or upcoming relevant decision. Route property professionals to the optional expert group. Exclude product insiders from the first-time usability sample. People researching only HDB transactions are outside this product's current scope; a separate discovery study could explore that need.

## Session format: 45 minutes

- 0–5 minutes: explain the study, obtain agreement for participation and any recording, and discuss their recent property research.
- 5–8 minutes: unprompted first impression. Ask: “What do you think this website helps you do? What would you try first?”
- 8–29 minutes: two persona-specific scenarios, allowing roughly 8–10 minutes each.
- 29–37 minutes: one recovery or limited-data scenario plus comprehension follow-ups.
- 37–45 minutes: debrief, compare with their current workflow and ask what would make them return.

Ask participants to think aloud. Give the scenario before showing control names or explaining the data. After silence or confusion, ask “What are you looking for?” or “What would you expect to happen?” If they remain blocked, record the point and assistance before helping. Separate spontaneous understanding from understanding after a targeted probe.

## Participant-facing scenarios and facilitator checks

The quoted prompt is for the participant. The checks underneath are for the facilitator and must not be read aloud before the task.

### A. Check an asking price — first-time buyer, upgrader

“You have seen a roughly 900–1,100 sq ft condo advertised for S$1.8 million in a development you're considering. Before arranging a viewing, use this website to decide whether the asking price deserves further investigation. Explain the evidence you would take into that conversation.”

Use a participant's known development if suitable; otherwise use a prechecked development with relevant sale records. The price is a hypothetical scenario, not a claim about that development.

Observe whether they find the development, select an appropriate recent period and size range, read supporting transactions, distinguish total price from $/sqft, and describe remaining uncertainty. Success is a reasoned answer grounded in the displayed records, not agreement that the price is fair. Ask afterward: “What does the headline price represent? Is it a valuation of this particular flat?”

### B. Choose between neighbourhoods — family upgrader

“Your household wants more space and easy access to daily essentials. You have two neighbourhoods in mind and a budget of S$2.2 million. Find one development in each that you would investigate further, then explain which better fits your household and why.”

Precheck candidate records at the hypothetical budget. Allow sequential comparison and spoken or written notes; do not imply that a dedicated comparison tool exists.

Observe how they combine price, size and map information, whether they discover amenity details and lifestyle priorities, and whether they understand that changing weights changes the score rather than the underlying neighbourhood. Ask afterward: “What would you still check about the journey to school or MRT?” Success includes recognising that proximity does not establish a walking route, complete amenity coverage or school-admission eligibility.

### C. Research rental-income potential — investor

“You are considering buying a private property to rent out. In an area you know, find two developments whose rental-income evidence you would investigate further. Explain which looks more promising and what additional information you need before choosing.”

Observe discovery of rental analytics, period selection, yield details and supporting sale/rental inputs. Allow sequential comparison. Success includes identifying gross yield as an estimate before costs and acknowledging that project-level evidence does not guarantee an individual unit's return.

After the task, ask: “What does this percentage include? What could make your actual return different? Why might a development have no yield figure?” Treat confusion between median project yields and a single pooled market yield as a comprehension issue to investigate.

### D. Prepare for a rent discussion — landlord

“Your tenant's lease is ending. You own a roughly 900–1,100 sq ft two-bedroom unit and want evidence for a rental discussion. Use this website to propose a monthly rent range you would discuss, and explain how comparable the evidence is.”

Use a prechecked development and adapt the size/bedroom brief if necessary. Do not tell them which controls to use.

Observe whether they choose rentals rather than sales, narrow by relevant period, size and bedrooms where possible, inspect individual contracts and distinguish monthly rent from rent per square foot. Success includes acknowledging differences among units and approximate floor-area bands. Ask afterward: “Which of these figures are estimates, and what would you verify elsewhere?”

### E. Assess a rental option — renter or relocating resident

“You have been quoted S$4,500 per month for a two-bedroom condo. You want convenient access to an MRT station, groceries and somewhere to eat. Use this website to decide whether to arrange a viewing, and tell us what you would check next.”

Assign a prechecked development with suitable rental and map data. The quoted rent is hypothetical.

Observe whether renter goals can be fulfilled despite investor terminology, whether they understand rental figures and explore nearby amenities. Success is a supported viewing decision with appropriate uncertainty. Ask afterward: “Would you rely on the journey estimate shown here? What does an amenity missing from the map tell you?”

### F. Recover from a restrictive search — all personas

“Your search isn't giving you enough useful evidence. Broaden it in a way that still fits your goal, then explain what changed.”

Start from a prechecked combination of location, short date range and tight size/price constraints that returns no or very few records. Do not manufacture a server failure or use a combination that takes too long to load.

Observe whether they notice active filters, alter a meaningful constraint, understand the scope of the new results and return to a useful state. Success requires recovery without silently losing track of the location or period. Distinguish genuine empty results from loading or service errors in notes.

### G. Interpret missing or approximate evidence — all personas

“You are researching this development, but some information is unavailable or approximate. What can you confidently conclude, and what would you do next?”

Precheck a sparse-data project with unavailable yield or an approximate-location project. If no suitable live case is accessible, use a clearly labelled static example and record it as a comprehension test rather than live usability evidence.

Observe whether they interpret N/A as unavailable/insufficient evidence rather than zero return, and whether an approximate marker suggests false precision. Success includes withholding a precise amenity conclusion when the location does not support one. Ask only after their first response: “Why do you think this number is missing?”

Alternate A/B and C/D ordering where feasible, and balance F/G coverage across the round. Adapt scenario details to participants' real goals without teaching the interface. Run one pilot session first to check task length and fixture suitability.

## Preparation

For each session, record the build/version, data snapshot or visible freshness, device and browser. Verify that the private test environment is reachable by the participant. Use hypothetical offers and rents rather than participants' personal finances.

Precheck and record the exact development, selected dates, usable records, relevant amenity information and expected state for each task. Prepare one sufficient-data case, one sparse-data case and one approximate-location case where available. Ground facilitator answers in that snapshot; do not invent current market numbers. Prepare reset states and confirm that leads and newsletter collection are disabled for this round.

After each task ask “How easy or difficult was that?” on a 1–7 scale and “How confident are you in your answer?” on a 1–5 scale. Compare confidence with observed correctness: a confident mistaken conclusion is especially useful evidence.

Capture the first click, the blocking point, mistaken assumptions and the evidence behind the final answer. Report raw counts per scenario because participants receive different tasks. Do not interpret a small qualitative sample as a population-wide percentage.

Prioritise findings as:

- Critical: the interface leads someone to a confident, materially misleading conclusion, such as treating gross yield as guaranteed net income or approximate proximity as school eligibility.
- Major: someone cannot complete a core task without directional help.
- Minor: friction that they recover from independently.

Treat even one serious misinterpretation as a finding to investigate. Group repeated issues across sessions, preserve supporting observations and distinguish product defects from research-fixture failures.

## Debrief questions

1. What decision did this website help you make, if any?
2. Which figure or feature felt most trustworthy? What made you think so?
3. Which information would you verify elsewhere, and how?
4. Where does this fit into the way you currently research properties?
5. Think of your next property-research task. Would you use this again? For what specific step?
6. What was the most confusing or missing part?

## Deliverable after the round

Produce a short findings report with participant mix, scenario outcomes, the five most consequential issues, direct observations, proposed changes and remaining questions. Revisit the provisional personas based on observed behaviour. Retest changed workflows with a fresh targeted sample before treating the issue as resolved.
